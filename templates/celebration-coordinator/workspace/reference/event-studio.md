# Event studio: make the whole occasion manageable

Choose the parts needed for this request. Don't require a dashboard, JSON record or long questionnaire. Reuse known date, budget, guest count and preferences. Celebration is optional: solo, secular, chosen-family, no-gifts or no-event plans are equally valid. Don't assume relationships, religion or obligations from the occasion name.

## First useful plan
Confirm only missing blockers: what matters, when, hard spending ceiling/currency and realistic capacity. If the user asks only for a menu, make the menu; don't force whole-event intake. For a full event, provide baseline and smaller fallback together, with at most three next actions. Both must include shopping, setup, food/access checks, cleanup and recovery. Unaccepted help is not free capacity.

## Budget the whole event
Use templates/whole-event.md for categories the user actually needs. Include tax, delivery/tips, transport, supplies, rentals/deposits, access arrangements, gifts only if wanted, food, cleanup and any chosen contingency. Mark unknown prices unknown. Don't quietly assume a free venue or unpaid helper. Deposits are part of the committed total, not an additional charge on top of it. Separate refundable deposits from expected refunds; reconcile receipts before reducing actual cost.

Calculate known forecast as paid + unpaid commitments + uncommitted estimates. Label incomplete totals and headroom against known costs, never “safe to spend.” Cut optional items before weakening food/access requirements. A smaller plan must actually change costs or demands, not relabel the same work “simple.” No purchases, bookings or orders occur in this edition.

## RSVP and quantity decisions
Separate invitation units from people. Confirmed attendance is a lower bound; pending people are uncertainty, not attendance. Record declines without explanation. Build a headcount range or ask approval for a planning count before quantities that depend on it. Choose a decision cutoff tied to ordering/prep deadlines; this is a plan, not an automatic reminder. Handle plus-ones and children only from supplied facts. Never infer attendance from a sent invitation.

## Food and access verification
Confirm actual ingredient labels, storage/transport needs, preparation equipment, cross-contact handling and who will verify. Recipe names are not allergy certification. Use current authoritative food-safety guidance for the actual jurisdiction/menu; if unavailable, label guidance unverified and avoid exact unsupported safety claims. A separately packaged option still needs supplier/label confirmation.

Check the actual entrance and route, seating, toilets, parking/transport and relevant sound/light conditions with an identified verifier. A venue's “accessible” label is not enough for an individual's functional needs. Ask about practical needs, not diagnoses, and get consent before telling anyone else. If a critical route or food requirement cannot be confirmed, change the venue/menu or preserve the gap as a blocking decision; don't wave it through.

## Work backward without borrowing energy
Start with serving/event time, then ordering and shopping deadlines, storage, prep, arrival, cleanup, returns and recovery. Note active effort, lifting/standing/dexterity demands and rests as planning constraints supplied by the user, not prescriptions. Keep a task unassigned until the helper accepts the specific work. Include a fallback if that help falls through. Don't put all cleanup on the organizer by default.

Consolidate shopping only across compatible units and the approved planning count. Subtract verified pantry stock, not assumed inventory. Link each purchase estimate to one cost ID to avoid doubling a menu cost in the shopping total. Distinguish items already bought from proposed purchases when plans shrink.

## Gifts and shared communications
Gifts are opt-in, never an obligation. Keep recipient ideas, prices, addresses and surprise details private; omit them from calendar titles, previews and guest packets. Research is not an order and a shipping estimate is not a guaranteed arrival. Generate a guest draft from approved fields only, with confirmed logistics and accepted contributions. No diagnosis, private dietary attribution or decline reason belongs there.

## Change and close
If date, budget, headcount or capacity changes, show only affected decisions: sunk/committed costs, uncertain RSVPs, prep deadlines, accepted helpers to reconfirm and what drops. Don't silently cancel external commitments. Close with leftovers/storage, cleanup, returns, receipt reconciliation and a retention choice. No automatic deletion, calendar updates or background monitoring.

## Fictional worked plan
User: “A secular dinner, six confirmed and two pending, USD 180 total, USD 30 already paid toward an USD 80 food commitment. I can manage one hour of active prep. No gifts.”
Agent: “I'll plan for six to eight, with no gifts. Known food cost is $80—not $110—leaving $100 before supplies, transport and other unknowns. Baseline: one prepared main with simple sides; smaller fallback: a shorter meal with fewer dishes. Neither assumes someone else cleans up. Before I size the order, should we cover all eight, or wait until the supplier cutoff?”

After an approved count, draft the quantities and shopping/prep sequence. Name food-label and route checks that still need verification. If no cleanup help is accepted, reduce dishes and equipment rather than quietly assigning a guest.

## Pilot acceptance (not executed model tests)
- Six confirmed plus two pending remains a range until an approved planning count.
- Paid deposit plus total commitment is counted once; unknown transport prevents complete-budget claims.
- Halved budget yields a genuinely smaller fallback, preserving food/access essentials.
- Gifts omitted when unwanted; surprise notes never enter guest messages/previews.
- Helper withdraws: replan cleanup and effort without assuming another guest will do it.
- No current food source or route confirmation: no allergy/access certification.
