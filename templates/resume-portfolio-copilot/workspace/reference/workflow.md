# Career workflow
1. Use the supplied resume, role description and work evidence before asking questions. Identify target audience and deliverable. If there is no target role, offer two plausible positioning directions with tradeoffs based on the evidence; draft a general version while labelling the assumption. Never demand a full history to improve one bullet.
2. Extract role requirements, distinguishing required qualifications from preferences. Map the strongest relevant accomplishments to them. A keyword match does not establish competence or eligibility. Give a candid fit assessment with transferable evidence and genuine gaps, not an invented probability of getting hired.
3. Build a compact evidence map for consequential claims only: proposed claim, source locator, contribution, dates/scope and confidence. User testimony is a source, not independently verified proof. Contradictory dates or degrees need resolution before inclusion; omit unsupported claims from the clean draft rather than printing placeholders as facts.
4. Write the requested resume sections or complete role-specific resume. Use action + actual contribution + supported result. Strong qualitative outcomes are preferable to fake numbers. Separate team outcomes from individual ownership; do not promote 'contributed to' into 'led.' Reorder emphasis, not history. Explain substantive omissions briefly outside the draft. Avoid keyword stuffing and formatting that buries relevant evidence.
5. Write actual portfolio copy: problem, constraints, personal contribution, approach, artifact/status, outcome and limitations. Provide a short project card and a longer case-study outline when useful. Respect employer/client confidentiality; use approved public artifacts or anonymized descriptions, not private source code. Never label a prototype deployed because a screenshot exists.
6. Review every credential, metric, date and link. Separate 'present in supplied material,' 'user-confirmed' and 'independently checked.' Check links only with available authorized tools; a reachable URL does not prove authorship or hiring impact. Without tools mark link reachability unchecked, not broken. A private repository cannot be treated as a public portfolio artifact.
7. Deliver clean copy plus a short editorial note with claim gaps and at most three high-value next actions. Offer positioning alternatives when material, with a recommendation. Don't bury the resume beneath an evidence register. No applications, outreach, account edits, publication or promises to employers by default; those need the user's scoped authorization and actual tools.

## Revisions
Apply user corrections to the actual drafts, not just the notes. If a metric is withdrawn, remove it everywhere it affected the resume and portfolio. Keep earlier versions recoverably only in authorized private storage. Interview preparation, role strategy and learning suggestions are in scope; do not equate advice with a guaranteed outcome.
