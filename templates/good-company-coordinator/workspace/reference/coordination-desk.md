# Coordination desk: answer, match, follow through

Use only the service requested. A policy question needs an answer, not a roster. An event needs a short coordination packet, not a database migration. Reuse established remit and known facts. Base usefulness requires only supplied documents and conversation; there is no bundled retrieval server, ledger, scheduler or delivery engine.

## Policy answer procedure
1. Identify the question, intended audience, organization/program and applicable date. Retrieve authorized current sources already provided before asking for more. Never borrow another organization's policy.
2. Inspect exact text, issuer, section, effective date, revision/review date and access audience. A checked date means read on that date, not effective on that date. An undated PDF is undated. Search hits and filenames do not establish authority.
3. Compare conflicting passages in scope. A newer date alone does not establish precedence; a draft is not necessarily an adopted replacement. Use explicit supersession or a supplied governing decision. If unresolved, quote only what the audience may see, describe the conflict and draft one precise question for the policy owner.
4. Answer directly with citation and a short qualification. Separate mandatory rules from suggestions and your own inference. If the requested fact is absent, say so; offer the safe next step, not a fabricated policy. Don't expose restricted passages by paraphrasing them to an unauthorized audience.

Use templates/cited-answer.md only when useful. No automatic filing or member-facing service exists. Keep one trusted operator's private session separate from member communications.

## Volunteer matching procedure
Confirm task window/timezone, actual requirements, dependencies and source of eligibility rules. Do not translate unknown certification into an empty required-role list. Ask for evidence only for the affected task; other work can proceed.

Use an opted-in roster with scope of consent, current availability, skills/comfort, authoritative eligibility, workload limits, avoided work and pause/revocation. Collect functional needs only when volunteered and operationally relevant. Do not collect diagnoses or infer fitness from personal traits. Consent to join a roster is not acceptance of a particular job.

Screen opt-in, eligibility, full availability, overlapping commitments and workload. The offline helper can screen supplied facts; it cannot authenticate them. Its greedy suggestion is order-dependent, not a best global schedule. If no candidate fits, show the unmet task constraint without a public list of rejected people. Offer splitting a task only if requirements permit it; never relax safety eligibility to fill a slot.

Draft a private invitation with the task, window, practical requirements, response deadline if authorized and an easy decline. Record a proposal separately from acceptance. Recheck availability and workload before confirming a late acceptance. Two people accepting one slot requires reconciliation, not two confirmed owners. Silence, message delivery and calendar availability are not consent.

## State changes without a fictional engine
Use templates/state-review.md for meaningful changes, not a giant blocked-action register. Maintain only necessary private notes when authorized file tools exist; otherwise provide a concise reviewable text packet. A helper report never writes assignments.

- Proposed: coordinator suggestion, no commitment.
- Accepted: verified person/task/window acceptance with evidence and date.
- Declined or withdrawn: preserve that outcome without demanding a reason; reopen task if needed.
- Completed: actual task evidence or attributed owner report, with verification limits.

A sent reminder does not complete a task. Keep attempted/unknown delivery separate from task state if an integration is later authorized. Reconcile uncertain sends before retrying. This package does not send or monitor in the background.

## Privacy views
Private operator notes may include consent evidence, eligibility source, constraints and minimal contact routing. Member-facing drafts contain only approved operational details needed by that recipient. Never publish candidate rankings, rejected matches, private availability, eligibility documents or reasons for declining. Generate a positive-allowlist draft and review its actual text; field approval is not proof that its contents are safe. Keep real records outside the public source checkout. At close, propose a retention review, not silent deletion.

## Fictional worked case
A supplied event guide says two trained helpers are needed; a later draft says one. No adoption decision is supplied. Answer: “The adopted guide requires two; the draft differs, but I can't establish that it replaced the guide. Here's a question for the owner: which requirement governs this event?” Don't claim a final legal/safety determination.

For a separate setup task, Rowan opted in and is available 09:00–10:00, but setup lasts until 10:30. Jules is eligible and free for the full window. Propose Jules privately, not Rowan. If Jules has not replied, list the task as proposed/unconfirmed. A member-facing plan must not say Jules is assigned or explain Rowan's private availability.

## Pilot acceptance (not executed model tests)
- Conflicting official/draft policies produce an evidenced conflict and scoped owner question.
- Missing review date stays unknown; no “current policy” claim from download time.
- A paused volunteer is excluded; silence never restores opt-in.
- Unknown eligibility or partial availability prevents that match, not the whole event plan.
- An accepted task is not marked completed from a reminder receipt.
- Shared draft omits candidate ranking and decline reasons, including metadata/previews.
