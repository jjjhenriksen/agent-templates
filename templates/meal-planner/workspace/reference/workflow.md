# Planning loop and record contract
1. Establish horizon, meal slots, servings, budget/currency, exclusions/allergens, available equipment and practical prep time. Use explicit tentative assumptions for unknown non-safety preferences. Don't invent safe substitutions for an unresolved allergy.
2. Inventory pantry/fridge/freezer with quantity, unit, condition and checked date. Unknown stock isn't zero confirmed stock. Expired or unsafe food is not usable inventory. Reserve stock once across the whole plan. Use fresh/perishable items early; keep food-safety dates separate from quality dates.
3. Choose source-linked recipes and base yields. Scale ingredient quantities by servings, not cooking temperatures or times. Separate ingredient need from package rounding. No cup-to-gram conversion without ingredient-specific evidence. Keep distinct preparations and allergen-specific products under separate IDs.
4. Draft a week with breakfast/lunch/dinner/snacks only where requested, ingredient reuse, a low-prep fallback, and intentional leftovers. Record which batch supplies each leftover meal; don't silently count it as both fresh cooking and leftover inventory. A batch must cover its original portions plus assigned leftovers. A changed meal regenerates shopping and leftover assignments.
5. Consolidate ingredient demand by exact ID and unit. Subtract usable pantry after other reservations, once, not once per recipe. Mark missing amounts/units. Quote prices only with store, timestamp and currency; otherwise label estimates. Fees, tax, substitutions and delivery are separate; no fabricated total.
6. Sequence thawing, cooking, cooling, storage, reheating and washing up. Check oven temperatures, burner occupancy, fridge space and active work conflicts. Replan rather than promise optimized scheduling. Use local authoritative food guidance; label cross-contact and supplier questions unresolved.
7. Deliver the meal grid, recipe links/yields, shop-by-aisle list, prep plan, leftover destinations and at most three decisions. Save source facts separately from calculated output. Reconcile actual groceries, costs and leftovers, not assumed delivery.

## Optional offline shopping helper
Python 3.11+, standard library. Run `python3 scripts/shopping.py examples/week.json`; JSON goes to stdout, no files or accounts changed. `need` is the already scaled whole-plan demand, not a recipe yield. Rows: ingredient ID (nonempty), unit (nonempty), need (nonnegative decimal string or null). Pantry rows: same ID/unit, available (decimal string), reserved (decimal string, defaults 0). Distinct IDs prevent accidental merging of different preparations/products. Duplicate pantry keys are rejected to prevent double counting. Unit groups never convert. Unknown needs remain unknown even when another recipe quantifies the same item; any shopping quantity for that group is only a lower bound. Every helper input is private; do not publish its raw report.

Meal records should also retain recipe source/yield, meal date/slot/servings, batch and leftover links, pantry checked dates, restrictions, budget estimates/actuals and unresolved questions. The helper intentionally validates only the shopping subrecord; it does not prove nutrition adequacy, safe storage, leftover sufficiency or schedule feasibility.
